# Blood tests

If you want hormone therapy, blood tests are always very important. We usually take blood tests before starting hormone therapy and again three months after starting. After that, we also monitor levels every 6–12 months, or whenever there are changes to the dosage. Typically, before starting hormone therapy, the following blood tests must be performed:

- Complete blood count (white blood cells, red blood cells, hemoglobin, platelets, hematocrit, mean corpuscular volume)
- Liver function tests (ALT, AST, ALP, bilirubin)
- Fasting blood glucose
- Urea and electrolytes / kidney panel (creatinine, eGFR, urea, potassium, sodium)
- Lipid/cholesterol panel (total cholesterol, triglycerides, HDL, LDL)
- Prolactin (PRL)
- Testosterone and estradiol (Total Testosterone and E2)

If you are undergoing feminizing hormone therapy and having follow-up blood tests, the following tests are usually required:

- Liver function tests (ALT, AST, ALP, bilirubin)
- Urea and electrolytes (U&E, renal function)
- Lipids (total cholesterol, triglycerides, HDL, LDL)
- Prolactin (PRL)
- Testosterone and estradiol (T and E2)

If you are undergoing masculinizing hormone therapy and having follow-up blood tests, the following tests are usually required:

- Complete blood count (FBC)
- Liver function tests (ALT, AST, ALP, bilirubin)
- Lipids (total cholesterol, triglycerides, HDL, LDL)
- Testosterone and estradiol (T, E2)

If you are taking [cyproterone acetate](https://min.medicin.dk/Medicin/Indholdsstoffer/318), it is very important that you pay particular attention to your prolactin levels. Cyproterone acetate carries a small risk of causing a [prolactinoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prolactinoma), a benign pituitary tumor. While this is not a common side effect of cyproterone acetate, it is important to consult your doctor if your prolactin levels are too high.

When it comes to getting blood tests done, you might be unsure of where to go, as your own GP most likely won't do them. In my experience, the best solution is to use [Dansk Sundhedsteam](https://ds-vt.dk/). You can order the tests through them; they then enter the order into the system, allowing you to book an appointment for the blood test at a hospital in your region. This is a great option because you don't have to visit their clinic; instead, you can have the tests done at a hospital near you. Once the samples are taken, they are sent to their laboratory. After processing, they send you the results digitally.

It can be a bit pricey, but in my experience, it is the cheapest option available. If you are receiving treatment at Imago, you can mention this to them, and they can then deduct the VAT from the price, helping you save some money.
