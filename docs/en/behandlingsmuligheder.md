# Treatment Options

As trans people in Denmark, we don't have as many options as in some other countries, but we still have different options for getting hormone therapy. The different options vary in price, availability, and ease of use. Here we will go through them and discuss their pros and cons.

## CKI

This is the most well-known option and probably also one you have already tried.

There are three different CKIs. They are located in Aalborg, Odense and Copenhagen. Every CKI is different and some are better than others.

CKI Aalborg is generally known for being the best of the three. It has a reputation for having better staff who treat trans people better and help them get hormones faster. CKI Aalborg can have a long wait before you can have your first conversation, but once you get started, it doesn't take an unreasonably long time to be approved for hormone therapy. If you want to start hormones at CKI, I would recommend getting a referral to Aalborg.

CKI Copenhagen has a reputation for treating trans people very badly, often asking uncomfortable questions, and that it feels like you are being interrogated by them. I suggest staying far away from them.

CKI Odense is generally not as bad as Copenhagen, but it is extremely slow. Many people experience waiting for several years before their case is taken up at conference, and even longer before they're approved for hormone therapy.

Whichever one you choose, there will be problems at CKI. CKI may be one of the cheapest ways to get hormone therapy, but it is also the slowest and most cumbersome. Many experience long waiting lists, discrimination and unpleasant conversations with employees. Because of these things, I wouldn't recommend CKI to most. If you have autism or mental disorders such as anxiety, OCD, depression, PTSD, etc., there is a high chance that they will reject you. At least a lot of people experience this, and that’s what I’ve experienced too.

If you want to start treatment at CKI, it may be a good idea to start the process there while using one of the other options until you are approved for hormone therapy at CKI.

## Private clinics

Private clinics can be one of the most expensive options you have, but it is also one of the best you have. Private clinics often give you quick access to treatment, various forms of hormones (gel, spray, pills, etc.) and give you EU-valid prescriptions so you get the hormones directly from a Danish pharmacy. There are various private clinics that can print EU-valid prescriptions. Below is a list of the most well-known private clinics:

- [Imago](https://www.imago.tg/) - Imago is probably the most well-known EU private clinic and offers good treatment at a reasonable price. This is the clinic I would recommend to most trans people.
- [Anne Health](https://anne.health/) - Anne Health is a new private clinic that has started in England, but has a positive reputation so far.
- [Hormony Gender](https://www.hormonygender.dk/) - Hormony Gender is the only Danish private clinic that offers hormone therapy for trans people and is also relatively new. Hormony Gender is not recommended because of their long and very costly process.
- [GenderGP](https://www.gendergp.com/) - GenderGP is not recommended. It used to be one of the most popular clinics but it absolutely no longer is. They have terrible service, are almost impossible to get in touch with and provide bad, sometimes completely wrong treatment. I'd stay far away from GenderGP.
  
## DIY

If you can't afford to or do not want to use a private clinic, your last option is DIY. But there are many different types of DIY. The most common and cheapest are injections. Injections are the best option for most people. They can be taken every 7-10 days, which means that a single bottle can last for a long time. They are also better at inhibiting the effect of testosterone so that one can do monotherapy, which means that one does not need to take antiandrogens. Because of this, injections are a cheap and effective solution.

For many, however, injections can be frightening or unpleasant. Fortunately, there are other solutions as well. Some of the sellers who sell injections also sell transdermal estrogen. Often in the shape of spray or gel. This is usually more expensive than injections, but if you can afford it, it can be a great alternative to injections. You can also buy estrogen pills, but these can also become reasonably expensive, and for some do not have as good effect, and can give higher risk of blood clots.

The biggest problem with DIY is that it can be really hard to find a seller who is not sold out. Unfortunately, this is just because there are many who need hormones and not as many who make them, and those who make them are not big companies but just some regular people who make it in their homes. Therefore, it is important to always keep an eye on who has something in stock. You may want to use websites such as [valerie.vg](https://valerie.vg/) or [diyhrt.coffee](https://diyhrt.coffee/) to keep track of who is selling what you need.
